Afterword - Ben Miller

P.92 The crucial stylistic decision to write in all lowercase lends the english-language text an air of 1970s informality bu tin German does something even more radical. German nouns are all capitalised: objects cruise through a sea of description and action like Prussian battleships in the Baltic Sea. Forcing everything into lowercase has the effect of confusing objects and actions. It emphasises process: it radically destabilises.

P.93 If politics are aesthetics — if we confuse the look and feel of resistance with he actual labour or organising, which has a lot to do with craft, in its own way — then nothing is politics, and nothing is aesthetics either. We rob ourselves both of our ability to act and to create, of our ability to organise and to describe.

P.102 Writing and thinking well are not acts of resistance any more than subculture is automatically resistant. They are not sufficient — but they are certainly necessary.
